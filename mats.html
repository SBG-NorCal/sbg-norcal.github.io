Uniting martial artists to build stronger communities.

MATS (Martial Arts Training and Scholarship) is the East Bay 501(c)(3) that powers SBG NorCal — and provides scholarships, summer camps, and long-term mentorship to underserved kids and young adults across our community.

Our mission

To support and nurture underserved children and young adults — who can realize enormous benefits from martial arts training and a supportive community.

MATS was founded in 2013 by a group of lifelong, committed martial artists from the East Bay. Our volunteers and coaches include former police officers, teachers, competitive athletes, and people from all walks of life. We teach kids practical skills not just for martial arts but for life outside the gym — like hard work, respect, and dedication.

How MATS works

Three pillars. One mission.

01

Scholarships

MATS provides martial-arts scholarships to underserved youth — both summer camps and extended year-round programs. Scholarship athletes train at local gyms with mentors and coaches who hold them to high standards.

02

Academic Support

We expect MATS members to maintain good grades, participate in regular training, and hone their life skills. Want to train more? Show us a commitment to academics and other responsibilities first.

03

Career Preparation

Resume writing, job interviewing, reliability, and showing up — the adult-life basics that get taken for granted. MATS pairs sponsored athletes with mentors who help them transition into the workforce.

A bridge to adult life

The long-term relationship that changes outcomes.

Many scholarship programs offer a weeklong camp and then send young adults back into the world. MATS fosters sponsored athletes over the long haul so they can develop meaningful relationships with adults, coaches, and mentors.

When a long-term relationship is established, young adults have a supportive framework that helps them address their life challenges — finishing school, applying for jobs, starting careers, and growing into the kind of adults their younger selves needed to see.

How do young adults join MATS?

MATS athletes are referred by their school, social-service agencies, or community members. Most start with an invitation to join an extended summer camp where they're exposed to training and our program. They can then join an extended summer program before transitioning into a long-term relationship.

Summer camp runs three days a week, three hours a day. Young adults train Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, help around a gym, and work on their mindfulness with a certified trainer.
